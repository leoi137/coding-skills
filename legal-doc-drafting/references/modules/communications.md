# Module: Email, SMS and push

**Load when** the product sends email beyond sign-in links, any SMS, or push notifications — and always when it sends messages to people who are not its users (a user's customers, invitees).

## Email (CAN-SPAM, 15 U.S.C. §7701 et seq.)
- **Commercial** messages (promoting the company): accurate headers and subject, identify as an ad where applicable, valid physical postal address, working unsubscribe honored within 10 business days, no selling of the list.
- **Transactional or relationship** messages (sign-in links, receipts, invoices a user asked the product to send, account notices): exempt from most requirements but must not have false or misleading routing information. Keep promotional content out of them or they can become commercial.
- **Messages a user sends through the product**: the user is generally the sender for CAN-SPAM purposes when they choose the recipient and content; the company may be an "initiator" if it adds its own promotional content (e.g., a "Powered by X — sign up" footer is low risk but should stay non-promotional in substance). Make the Terms assign recipient-consent responsibility to the user and prohibit bulk unsolicited use.
- Open/click tracking and read receipts (e.g., "viewed" status) are personal-data processing about recipients — disclose in the privacy policy.

## SMS (TCPA, 47 U.S.C. §227; carrier 10DLC rules)
- Marketing texts by autodialer need **prior express written consent** with required disclosures; informational texts need prior express consent. Statutory damages are $500–$1,500 per message — the highest per-violation exposure most apps face.
- Required program terms: program name, message frequency, "Msg & data rates may apply," STOP to opt out, HELP for help, link to terms and privacy. Carriers require the privacy policy to state that mobile numbers and SMS consent are not shared with third parties for marketing.
- State mini-TCPAs (FL, OK, others) add rules for marketing texts. Don't draft an SMS section unless an SMS program is live.

## Push notifications
OS-level opt-in; describe the categories sent and how to turn them off. Marketing push to minors and late-night sends are emerging regulatory topics; avoid promising frequency limits you don't enforce.
