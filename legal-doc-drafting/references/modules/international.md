# Module: Users outside the United States

**Load when** the product markets to or meaningfully serves users outside the US. Otherwise, a short "intended for the US; if you use it from elsewhere, your data is processed in the US" paragraph is enough — and true geographic targeting (no EU-language marketing, no EU pricing, no EU-specific features) keeps most foreign law out of reach.

## EU / UK GDPR (applies when offering services to, or monitoring, people in the EU/UK — Art. 3(2))
- **Article 13/14 information:** controller identity and contact; DPO if appointed; purposes **and lawful basis for each** (contract, legitimate interests with the interest named, consent, legal obligation); recipients; transfers and safeguards; retention per category; rights (access, rectification, erasure, restriction, portability, objection, withdraw consent, complain to a supervisory authority); whether provision is required; automated decision-making.
- **Representative** (Art. 27 / UK equivalent) for non-EU controllers unless processing is occasional and low-risk.
- **Transfers:** vendors certified under the EU–US Data Privacy Framework (check the DPF list for each vendor) or SCCs; disclose which.
- **Cookies:** ePrivacy/PECR requires prior consent for non-essential cookies — a US "notice-only" approach doesn't work for EU users.
- **Processor contracts (Art. 28)** with every vendor; offer a DPA to B2B customers who put their own customers' data in the product.

## Canada
PIPEDA (meaningful consent, accountability, openness) and **Quebec Law 25** (privacy officer named publicly, privacy impact assessments for transfers outside Quebec, consent for profiling/tracking technologies off by default).

## Others
Brazil LGPD, Australia Privacy Act, Japan APPI, India DPDP Act — load the specific law when a launch targets that country; don't scatter a sentence about each into a US policy.
