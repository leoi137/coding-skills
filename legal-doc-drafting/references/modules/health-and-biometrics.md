# Module: Health and biometric data

**Load when** the product collects anything that reveals physical or mental health (including inferences from purchases, location near clinics, fitness or cycle data), or face/voice/fingerprint templates.

- **HIPAA** applies only to covered entities (providers, plans, clearinghouses) and their business associates. Consumer apps usually aren't — don't claim "HIPAA compliant" unless a BAA relationship exists; say what you actually do.
- **FTC Health Breach Notification Rule** (16 C.F.R. Part 318, amended 2024): non-HIPAA health apps drawing data from multiple sources must notify users and the FTC of breaches, and an unauthorized disclosure (e.g., sharing with an ad platform via pixel) counts as a breach.
- **Washington My Health My Data Act** (private right of action), **Nevada SB 370**, **Connecticut** consumer-health-data provisions: separate consumer-health-data privacy policy linked from the homepage (WA requires this), opt-in consent to collect and separate consent to share, a written authorization to sell, and geofencing bans around health facilities. These apply far below typical state-law thresholds.
- **Wellness and coaching products** (habits, sleep, mood, fitness): habit names and notes can reveal health conditions, which pulls them into MHMDA-style "consumer health data" even when the product isn't medical. Pick a position (usually: treat as consumer health data, publish the WA policy, get opt-in consent) and draft a "not medical advice, not a medical device, call 911/988 in an emergency" line. AI that behaves like a therapist is restricted in some states (e.g., Illinois's 2025 Wellness and Oversight for Psychological Resources Act; Nevada AB 406) — verify scope before describing an AI coach.
- **Biometrics:** Illinois BIPA (private right of action, statutory damages) requires a public retention/destruction policy, written notice, and written release before collection; Texas CUBI and Washington's biometric law add their own rules. Many state comprehensive laws treat biometrics as sensitive (opt-in). Avoid biometric features unless essential; if present, draft the BIPA policy and consent flow before launch.
