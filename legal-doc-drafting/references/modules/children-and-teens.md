# Module: Children and teens

**Load when** the product is directed to under-18s, has a mixed audience, has a store age rating below 17+/Mature, or the facts suggest minors use it. For adult-only products, the base privacy section ("not for people under 18; we delete information we learn came from a child under 13") is enough.

- **COPPA** (amended rule fully in force 2026-04-22; verify): applies to services directed to children under 13 or with actual knowledge of collecting from them. Requires a direct notice to parents, verifiable parental consent before collection, separate consent before disclosing children's data to third parties (including for advertising), a written information-security program, a written retention policy, and limits on using persistent identifiers. Directed-to-children analysis looks at subject matter, visual content, animated characters, audience composition, and ads. If the product is not built for children, don't build COPPA machinery — exclude under-13s and act on actual knowledge.
- **Teens (13–17):** state privacy laws treat known 13–15/13–17 data as sensitive or ban targeted advertising/sale without consent (e.g., CT, CO, NJ, MD, NY Child Data Protection Act); state age-appropriate design codes and social-media laws are in active litigation (verify current status before relying).
- **App-store accountability acts:** see `mobile-apps.md`.
- **Contract capacity:** minors can disaffirm; require 18+ for paid or contract-forming products.
