# House Style

The output is a finished legal document the company will publish. It should read as if competent counsel wrote it for this product: declarative, precise, plain where it can be, formal only where formality carries legal weight.

## Voice
- **Plain English first.** "We don't sell your personal information" beats "The Company shall not effectuate the sale of Personal Information." Keep legal formality for defined terms, conspicuous disclaimers, and phrases with settled meaning ("to the maximum extent permitted by law").
- **Second person for the user, first-person plural for the company.** Define the parties once.
- **No marketing.** Legal pages make no promises of outcomes ("you'll always get paid," "bank-level security"). Promises are representations; representations are liability.
- **Short sentences, short sections.** A reader should find the cancellation rule or the deletion process in seconds. Lead each section with the rule; follow with detail.
- **Summaries are fine, but they bind too.** A plain-language summary at the top must be consistent with the detailed text; courts read ambiguities against the drafter.

## Defined terms
Define on first use, capitalize thereafter, use identically across Terms and Privacy. Don't define a term you never reuse. Name each user type (e.g., "Contractor," "Client") and use those names throughout.

## Conspicuousness
- Warranty disclaimers and liability limits: visually distinct (all caps or bold), not buried.
- Arbitration: a plain-language notice at the top of the Terms pointing to the section; the jury-trial waiver in conspicuous text.
- Auto-renewal terms: clear and conspicuous, near the price.

## Structure
- Numbered sections with stable anchors. Group related rules (all billing together, all dispute rules together).
- "Last updated" date bumped on every substantive change, from the repo's single source for it.
- Length is earned: every section must trace to a fact in the ledger or a legal requirement. Typical lean results: Privacy 1,800–3,500 words; Terms 3,000–6,000 words for a two-sided product.

## Placeholders
If a sentence can't be finished without a fact, finish everything else and leave one visible marker: `[FACT-TO-VERIFY: what you need]`. Never ship a silent guess. Placeholders must be resolved (or the sentence cut) before publishing.
